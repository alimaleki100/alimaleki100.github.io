I led the data product workstream behind a SuperApp feature that brought orders from multiple Snapp ventures into one customer experience.

The product problem

One place for orders across the SuperApp.

Customers could place orders through distinct Snapp ventures—such as Cab, Box, Shop, Market and Doctor—but the SuperApp needed a unified Order Center experience.

That front-end experience required a dependable data backbone capable of collecting, standardizing and serving order data from independently operated ventures.

Responsibility boundary

I owned the data side—not the application build.

My accountability covered the data product and the team delivering it. The application feature was owned by a separate development organization.

In scope
  • Data pipelines
  • ClickHouse storage
  • APIs & data services
  • Data-quality monitoring
Outside scope
  • Mobile UI
  • App feature development
  • Customer-facing design
  • Operational reporting
Data architecture

My role

Data Product Manager

I led the five-engineer data team, shaped the backlog and coordinated delivery with application product managers and team leads.

  • Translated the application need into data-product capabilities and interfaces.
  • Managed priorities, dependencies and Scrum delivery for the data workstream.
  • Aligned the application and data teams on readiness, contracts and integration.
Critical delivery risk

Not every venture was ready to provide data.

The central feature depended on multiple autonomous ventures. Source readiness became a product dependency, not only an engineering issue.

I coordinated with venture and application counterparts to expose blockers, sequence integrations and keep the central workstream moving.

Verified outcome

A production data product at SuperApp scale.

Production foundation

ClickHouse storage and pipelines built from scratch for cross-venture order data.

Application enablement

APIs and data services made order information available to the application team.

Operational confidence

Quality monitoring and observability supported a data estate of hundreds of millions of records.
